### 1. The Second and Third Decade After 103Pd Plaque Brachytherapy for Choroidal Melanoma.
- Authors: Margarita Labkovich et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-14 | PMID: 42874810 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42874810/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ophtha.2026.10.009

**Abstract (EN)**

To determine long-term visual and ocular outcomes in a large cohort of patients with choroidal melanoma treated using palladium-103 (103Pd) plaque brachytherapy.
This is a retrospective cohort study.
170 patients with a minimum of 10 years of continuous follow-up at The New York Eye Cancer Center following 103Pd plaque brachytherapy treatment for choroidal melanoma.
The study includes collected data consisting of patient demographic characteristics, tumor features, such as size, location, and AJCC clinical T-category (cT), visual outcomes, variables related to post-treatment retinopathy management, and long-term ocular comorbidities.
The primary endpoints were tumor control, ocular conservation, and visual acuity. Secondary endpoints included long-term radiation-related complications, additional ocular interventions, and associated comorbidities. Statistical analyses were performed using the Kaplan-Meier survival method, t-tests, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, chi-square tests, and linear regression plots.
Among 170 patients with a minimum 10-year follow-up there was 100% local tumor control. One eye (0.6%) was enucleated due to neovascular glaucoma and patient choice. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated visual acuity retention rates of 92.7% at 12 years, 87.0% at 15 years, and 63.7% at 20 years defined as vision of 20/200 or better. Radiation retinopathy was managed with anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy, intravitreal corticosteroids, and laser treatment. Tumors located closer to the fovea were associated with increased foveal radiation doses and poorer visual acuity outcomes. Treatment of juxtapapillary and circumpapillary tumors was associated with the highest rates of vision loss.
This study is the largest and longest follow-up analysis of 103Pd plaque brachytherapy for choroidal melanoma. It provides new long-term information on tumor control, ocular morbidity, and visual outcomes that are valuable for patient counseling and therapeutic decision-making.

### 1. Clinical outcomes of epiretinal membrane surgery in patients with good baseline visual acuity.
- Authors: Rebecca L Jones et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-14 | PMID: 42870525 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42870525/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000004976

**Abstract (EN)**

For patients with symptomatic epiretinal membrane (ERM), pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) with ERM peel is often performed to improve their distortion. However, there is no clear consensus on visual acuity threshold for performing surgery, nor evidence of potential likelihood of risk to allow patients to make informed decisions.
A retrospective case notes review of visual outcomes and complications was performed at 4 UK surgical centres. All patients with symptomatic ERM and Snellen best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 20/40 or better (LogMAR 0.3 or better), were included. Outcome measures were BCVA at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, as well as subjective improvement in symptoms, complications and further surgery.
163 eyes of 163 patients were included in analysis. For this cohort, the mean pre-operative BCVA was equivalent to the mean post-operative BCVA. 80% (n=133) maintained or improved from their baseline VA of ≤20/40, whereas 1.2% (n=2) suffered major harm with post-operative BCVA of worse than 20/200. 92% of patients reported symptomatic improvement following surgery.
This study shows that for the vast majority of patients with symptomatic ERM but good visual acuity, pars plana vitrectomy and epiretinal membrane peel is a safe and successful surgery.
