### 2. Three-dimensional architecture of outer retinal tubulation in Bietti crystalline dystrophy.
- Authors: Maasa Ogata et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-05 | PMID: 42833384 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833384/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.10.001

**Abstract (EN)**

To characterize the three-dimensional morphology and cellular-level organization of outer retinal tubulation (ORT) in Bietti crystalline dystrophy (BCD) and to evaluate its structural relationship with the residual outer retina.
Observational case series.
Patients with BCD exhibiting ORT were included.
ORT and contiguous ORT-associated regions were evaluated three-dimensionally using two complementary approaches: en face optical coherence tomography (OCT) images generated from the innermost ORT boundary to Bruch's membrane, and ellipsoid zone (EZ)-like band-to-Bruch's membrane thickness maps (hereafter referred to as EZ-to-Bruch's membrane thickness maps). Cellular-level ORT organization was further assessed using adaptive optics OCT (AO-OCT).
Three-dimensional ORT morphology, spatial relationship between ORT-associated structures and the EZ-like band, EZ-to-Bruch's membrane thickness distribution, and AO-OCT features of ORT wall organization were assessed.
Nineteen eyes from 19 patients were included. En face OCT images were analyzable in 15 eyes, and AO-OCT images were analyzable in 9 eyes. The EZ-like band could be traced to the hyperreflective ORT wall in all eyes assessed using en face OCT. En face OCT showed complex ORT-associated planar structures with branching and confluent configurations. EZ-to-Bruch's membrane thickness maps closely corresponded to the contours of these ORT-associated planar structures, suggesting that the maps reflected the overall geometry of the residual outer retina. Peripheral EZ-to-Bruch's membrane thickening was observed in most eyes, and quantitative thickness-distance analysis identified characteristic peripheral peak patterns in eyes with intermediate-sized ORT-associated regions, suggesting that these thickened regions may be associated with ORT formation. AO-OCT showed multiple hyperreflective dots arranged in rosette-like configurations around a central hyporeflective lumen, consistent with presumed photoreceptor nuclei within the ORT wall.
ORT in BCD showed complex three-dimensional planar configurations spatially associated with the residual outer retina. These findings suggest that the peripheral margin of the ORT-associated outer retina may represent a key site of structural reorganization and support the concept that ORT in BCD represents a manifestation of outer retinal structural reorganization in the setting of progressive degeneration.

### 3. Interpretable Machine Learning for Course Prediction in Central Serous Chorioretinopathy Using Baseline OCT from a Multicenter Cohort.
- Authors: Lorenzo Ferro Desideri et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-08 | PMID: 42849862 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42849862/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.10.002

**Abstract (EN)**

To predict disease clinical course in central serous chorioretinopathy (CSC) using an interpretable machine learning model applied to baseline artificial intelligence (AI)-derived optical coherence tomography (OCT) biomarkers acquired at first presentation.
Multicenter, retrospective cohort study.
Overall, 329 treatment-naïve CSC eyes recruited across eight international centers in Europe, the Middle East, Asia, and North Africa.
Treatment-naïve CSC patients were retrospectively included and classified, based on follow-up, as acute or chronic CSC according to subretinal fluid (SRF) resolution. Baseline OCT biomarkers were extracted using an AI-based platform (Discovery OCT Biomarker Detector; RetinAI AG, Switzerland), which quantified retinal layer thicknesses, including the outer nuclear layer (ONL) and photoreceptor-retinal pigment epithelium (PR+RPE) complex, fluid volumes (SRF and RPE detachment [PED]), and biomarker probabilities, across central, parafoveal, and perifoveal ETDRS regions. These features used to develop an interpretable machine learning model predicting future CSC chronicity.
Classification of eyes as acute or chronic CSC, determined by SRF resolution on follow-up; discriminative performance of the Random Forest classifier, assessed by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC); and biomarker importance, evaluated using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values.
Of 329 eyes (291 patients), 92 (28.0%) were classified as acute and 237 (72.0%) as chronic CSC; mean follow-up was 4.2 ± 1.6 years. Chronic CSC patients were significantly older (51.7 ± 12.6 vs. 43.2 ± 9.6 years, p<0.001) with worse baseline visual acuity (0.25 ± 0.21 vs. 0.16 ± 0.15 logMAR, p=0.029). Among baseline OCT biomarkers, parafoveal ONL thickness showed the strongest association with disease chronicity (q<0.0001; r=-0.414). The Random Forest classifier achieved an AUC of 0.852 (95% CI: 0.748-0.940). SHAP analysis identified parafoveal ONL thickness, perifoveal PR+RPE thickness, and central SRF volume as the strongest predictors of acute CSC, whereas greater RNFL thickness, PED volume, FLAT PED probability, and HF probability were the most influential predictors of chronic disease.
Automatically detected OCT biomarkers can predict CSC chronicity at initial presentation with good discriminatory performance. Quantitative markers of outer retinal integrity, fluid configuration, and RPE dysfunction may improve prognostication, facilitate patient counseling, and help identify individuals at increased risk of persistent CSC.

### 1. Mitomycin C Application during Trabeculectomy-Injection versus Sponge: A Report by the American Academy of Ophthalmology.
- Authors: Michele C Lim et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-07 | PMID: 42841846 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42841846/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ophtha.2026.07.042

**Abstract (EN)**

To evaluate the recently published literature on the efficacy and safety outcomes of delivering mitomycin C (MMC) by either sub-Tenon's/subconjunctival injection (MMC-I) or by sponge (MMC-S) during trabeculectomy surgery.
A literature search was last conducted in the PubMed database in May 2026 and yielded 135 articles. Eighteen full-text articles were reviewed, and 11 met all inclusion criteria for this assessment and were assigned a level of evidence rating by the panel methodologist.
Six studies were rated level I, 2 were rated level II, and 3 were rated level III. The racial and ethnic backgrounds of study participants represented a diverse mix, and in many studies, a variety of glaucoma diagnoses were included, the majority of which were primary open-angle glaucoma. The MMC-I technique was reported to have used a wider range of drug concentrations (0.05-0.4 mg/mL) than MMC-S (0.1-0.4 mg/mL). The complete success rate of intraocular pressure (IOP) control ranged from 40.3% to 90.9% in the MMC-I group compared with 17.7% to 86.5% in the MMC-S group. The wide range of success rates may be a result of differences in definitions of success, follow-up time, and patient populations. Change in IOP from baseline ranged from 2.8 mmHg (17%) to 19.4 mmHg (58%) in the MMC-I group compared with 5.8 mmHg (31%) to 18.0 mmHg (59%) in the MMC-S group. No significant differences in complications, such as rates of bleb leak, hypotony, choroidal effusion, or choroidal hemorrhage, were reported. Two studies reported significantly fewer 5-fluorouracil injections in the MMC-I group, and 1 reported more glaucoma surgery reoperations in the MMC-S group. Several studies reported a more favorable bleb morphology that was diffuse, lower profile, and less vascular in the MMC-I group than in the MMC-S group.
Although MMC-I may offer some advantages over MMC-S trabeculectomy with respect to bleb morphology, level I and II evidence supports the application of MMC using either injection or sponges during trabeculectomy. Both applications result in similar rates of surgical success (a lower IOP and a reduction in medication burden) and have a similar safety profile.

### 2. Prevalence of Diabetic Retinopathy and Cardiovascular Risk Factors Among Diverse Hispanic/Latino Adults: HCHS/SOL and SOL Ojos Results.
- Authors: Lawrence J Ulanski et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-09 | PMID: 42854854 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854854/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ophtha.2026.09.037

**Abstract (EN)**

To estimate diabetic retinopathy (DR) prevalence among Hispanic/Latino adults with diabetes, assess heterogeneity by background group, and evaluate cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors.
Cross-sectional analysis of SOL Ojos, a multicenter ancillary study embedded within the population-based Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL).
Adults aged ≥40 years completing HCHS/SOL Visit 3 (V3; 2020-2024) from Chicago and Miami were examined (3,294/3,864; 85.2%). The analytic sample included 1,224 participants with diabetes and gradable retinal imaging (mean age, 62.7 years [95% CI, 61.4-64.0]; 48.6% female).
Participants underwent standardized ophthalmic examinations with centrally graded ultra-widefield fundus imaging. Major CVD risk factors (diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, obesity, smoking) were assessed at V3 and analyzed individually and cumulatively. DR, macular edema (ME), and vision-threatening DR (VTDR) were graded using Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) protocols. Survey-weighted analyses estimated age-standardized prevalence, and sequentially adjusted logistic regression evaluated associations.
Age-standardized prevalence of DR outcomes and associations with CVD risk factors.
Age-standardized any DR prevalence was 34.7% (95% CI, 29.2-40.2). Among men, any DR and VTDR prevalence was 41.8% (95% CI, 33.5-50.0) and 13.4% (95% CI, 7.4-19.5), versus 27.2% (95% CI, 19.5-34.9) and 9.4% (95% CI, 1.4-17.4) among women. Any DR ranged across Hispanic/Latino backgrounds from 13.9% (95% CI, 1.8-26.0) to 55.4% (95% CI, 49.2-61.6) in men and 15.3% (95% CI, 4.4-26.2) to 39.2% (95% CI, 22.3-56.1) in women, with the highest estimates among Cuban individuals. CVD risk factors were common and clustered (≥4: men 42.7% [95% CI, 34.5-50.9]; women 35.6% [95% CI, 29.3-41.9]). In adjusted models, hypertension was associated with any DR (OR, 3.16 [95% CI, 1.76-5.65]) and VTDR (OR, 2.86 [95% CI, 1.05-7.79]). Overall cumulative CVD risk burden was not significantly associated with VTDR (Type III F test, P=0.1193), despite adjustment for diabetes duration; the 3-risk-factor category had higher estimated odds than the 1-risk-factor category (OR, 5.10 [95% CI, 1.22-21.39]).
In this cross-sectional study of Hispanic/Latino adults with diabetes, more than one-third had DR, with substantial heterogeneity across background groups. Hypertension was associated with DR outcomes, particularly VTDR. Findings provide contemporary prevalence estimates and support longitudinal evaluation of cardiometabolic risk and DR outcomes.

### 1. Incidence of Retinal Artery Occlusion After Carotid Endarterectomy Versus Carotid Artery Stenting.
- Authors: Zuhair Zaidi et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-06 | PMID: 42854197 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854197/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005029

**Abstract (EN)**

Retinal artery occlusion (RAO) is a rare but vision-threatening complication of carotid revascularization, yet its comparative incidence after carotid endarterectomy (CEA) versus carotid artery stenting (CAS) is poorly characterized. We compared the risk of RAO and associated neurologic outcomes after CEA and CAS.
Retrospective multicenter cohort study using the TriNetX Research Network (January 1, 2004-March 15, 2026). Adults undergoing CEA or CAS with ≥6 months of follow-up and no prior diagnosis of any studied outcome condition (retinal artery or vein occlusion, glaucoma, legal blindness, or stroke were included. Propensity score matching on demographics, comorbidities, ophthalmic conditions, medications, and laboratory biomarkers (nearest-neighbor greedy algorithm; caliper 0.25 SD) yielded 17,851 patients per cohort. The primary outcome was the incidence of RAO at 90 days and 1 year after the index procedure. Secondary outcomes included central RAO (CRAO), branch RAO (BRAO), retinal vein occlusion, glaucoma, legal blindness, stroke (stratified by National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score), and mortality at the same intervals. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated.
At 1 year, CAS was associated with higher risks of RAO (0.421% vs 0.257%; OR 1.64; 95% CI, 1.12-2.41), CRAO (OR 2.30; 1.10-4.84), BRAO (OR 3.21; 1.58-6.52), and legal blindness (OR 1.59; 1.26-2.92). CAS also conferred higher odds of stroke (6.20% vs 4.43%; OR 1.42; 1.25-1.62) and mortality (6.41% vs 3.87%; OR 1.70; 1.54-1.88). Most events occurred within 72 hours post-procedure.
In this retrospective propensity-matched analysis, CAS was associated with greater risk of retinal and neurologic ischemic complications than CEA, although absolute event rates were low. These observational findings support consideration of heightened postoperative ophthalmic vigilance and interdisciplinary collaboration between ophthalmology and vascular surgery after CAS and suggest that targeted post-procedural ophthalmic evaluation may be reasonable in selected high-risk patients, pending prospective confirmation.

### 2. Long-Term Foveal Microvascular Alterations in Children With Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy: An OCT Angiography Study.
- Authors: Zeynep Seymen et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-06 | PMID: 42854202 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854202/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000004974

**Abstract (EN)**

To investigate the foveal microvascular structure in children with a history of hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) compared with healthy controls.
This prospective cross-sectional comparative study included 46 eyes of 23 children with HIE and 40 eyes of 20 age-matched healthy controls. The children were grouped as follows: Group 1: HIE Stage I (n=28 eyes); Group 2: HIE Stage II-III (n=18 eyes); Group 3: healthy age-matched controls (n=40 eyes). Patients were also subgrouped based on fundus appearance within the first week of life as follows: Group A: eyes without any retinal hemorrhages (RHs) (n=13 eyes); Group B: eyes with widespread peripheral RHs sparing the macula (n=28 eyes); Group C: eyes with widespread peripheral RHs with macular involvement (n=5 eyes); Group D: healthy controls (n=40 eyes). All children underwent optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA)(Solix Visionix). FAZ area, foveal vessel densities and central foveal thickness (CFT) were compared.
Foveal vessel density of the superficial capillary plexus (VD-SCP) and CFT were significantly higher in Group 2 compared with Group 1 (all p<0.05). Significantly smaller FAZ area was noted in Group 2 (0.19±0.09 mm2) compared with Group 1 (0.28±0.10 mm2) and 3 (0.26±0.09 mm2)(all p<0.05).
HIE may lead to persistent alterations in foveal microvasculature. OCTA may serve as a non-invasive biomarker for detecting long-term retinal microvascular sequelae in children with a history of perinatal asphyxia.

### 3. Pooled Data Meta-Analysis of Pneumatic Vitreolysis for Treatment of Vitreomacular Traction Syndrome With, And Without Full Thickness Macular Holes.
- Authors: Nathan A Brice et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-06 | PMID: 42854198 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854198/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005028

**Abstract (EN)**

To conduct a pooled data meta-analysis assessing the efficacy and complication rates of pneumatic vitreolysis as a first-line treatment for vitreomacular traction with and without full-thickness macular holes (FTMH).
The National Library of Medicine PubMed interface, EMBASE, Scopus, and Cochrane Library were queried for articles published from May 1995 to March 2025 detailing the use of a gas injection for treatment of vitreomacular traction with or without FTMH. Efficacy and complication data were pooled and analyzed.
A total of 33 studies reporting on 637 eyes with VMT treated with PVL were included. Of these, 476 eyes had VMT alone and 161 had concurrent FTMH. VMT release occurred in 76% of all eyes with rates of 49%, 67%, 71%, and 83%, for filtered air, SF6, C2F6 and C3F8 respectively. Across eyes with VMT alone, the release rate was 71%, while eyes with VMT and macular holes (MH) it was 94%, and successful MH closure rate was 40%. VMT release was higher in phakic (84%) compared to pseudophakic eyes (76%). Adverse events included development of new FTMHs in 5% of VMT-only eyes, and across all studies, retinal tears and detachments developed in 8 (1%) and 13 (2%) eyes, respectively.
Pneumatic vitreolysis appears to be a cost-effective treatment option for patients with vitreomacular traction without FTMH with modest to good success rates. Eyes with concurrent macular hole have a modest closure rate only. The risk of complications, including FTMH, retinal tear or detachment, must be considered.

### 1. Association between varicose veins and central serous chorioretinopathy.
- Authors: Bertan Cakir et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-07 | PMID: 42843970 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42843970/) | DOI: 10.1136/bjo-2026-330067

**Abstract (EN)**

AimTo investigate the association between varicose veins and central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR), based on emerging genetic evidence of a shared vascular mechanism.
Propensity score-matched retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network. Patients with varicose veins (n=621 513) were matched 1:1 to controls without varicose veins on age, sex, race and ethnicity, with prior CSCR or serous pigment epithelial detachment (PED) excluded. A secondary analysis excluded glucocorticoid users and additionally matched on hypertension, sleep apnoea and healthcare utilisation. A tertiary analysis compared thromboembolic risk (pulmonary embolism (PE) and deep vein thrombosis (DVT)) in varicose vein patients with and without CSCR.
Varicose veins were significantly associated with CSCR (risk ratio (RR) 2.16; 95% CI 1.86 to 2.52; p<0.0001) and serous PED (RR 1.97; 95% CI 1.69 to 2.93; p<0.0001). Associations persisted after excluding glucocorticoid users and adjusting for comorbidities (CSCR RR 1.83; 95% CI 1.22 to 2.75; p=0.003; PED RR 1.95; 95% CI 1.38 to 3.34; p=0.013). Among varicose vein patients, rates of DVT (RR 1.36; p=0.078) and PE (RR 1.41; p=0.14) did not differ significantly between those with and without CSCR.
Varicose veins were significantly associated with CSCR and serous PED, independent of glucocorticoid exposure and known comorbidities, supporting a possible systemic vascular mechanism in CSCR pathophysiology.

### 2. Retinal nerve fibre layer thickness change in the parapapillary region.
- Authors: Jost B Jonas et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-07 | PMID: 42843969 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42843969/) | DOI: 10.1136/bjo-2026-330479

**Abstract (EN)**

To assess radial changes in the retinal nerve fibre layer thickness (RNFLT) in the temporal parapapillary region.
Using radial optical coherence tomographic images of the optic nerve head, we measured in a sample of participants of the population-based Beijing Eye Study the RNFLT in the temporal horizontal parapapillary region at locations 'A' (intact inner (INL) and outer (ONL) nuclear layer), 'B' (INL/ONL end), 'C' (just beyond the INL/ONL end) in the parapapillary gamma zone centre and at the disc border. Exclusion criterion was any optic nerve damage including glaucoma.
The study included 47 eyes (47 participants). RNFLT increased (p<0.001) from 86±24 µm at 'A' (optic disc centre distance (ODCD): 1523±477 µm) to 128±42 µm at 'B' (ODCD: 1192±394 µm) and to 190±64 µm at 'C' (ODCD: 1023±312 µm). The increasing RNFLT measurements from location 'A' to 'B' by 49% and from 'B' to 'C' by 49% were double as high as geometrically expected ('A' to 'B': 1523 µm/1192 µm (28%); 'B' to 'C': 1192 µm/1023 µm (17%)). In the temporal parapapillary gamma zone (detected in 23 eyes), RNFLT changed from the mid of gamma zone (ODCD: 1069±291 µm) to the disc border (ODCD: 618±228 µm) from 210±76 µm to 238±81 µm (or +13%), while the ODCD decreased by 42%.
RNFLT in the temporal horizontal parapapillary region markedly increased just beyond (in direction to the disc border) of the INL/ONL end while within gamma zone RNFLT changed only marginally. Absence of underlying INL/ONL was associated with pronounced RNFL thickening, without major further radial RNFLT changes in gamma zone. These findings may be of importance for RNFLT measurements in highly myopic eyes.
