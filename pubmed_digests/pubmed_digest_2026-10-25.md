### 1. Risk Factors for Retinal Pigment Epithelium Tears in Patients with Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
- Authors: Salem Abu Al-Burak et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-19 | PMID: 42896642 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42896642/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.10.007

**Abstract (EN)**

To identify and synthesize clinical, imaging, and treatment-related risk factors associated with retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) tears in neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).
RPE tears are a vision-threatening complication of nAMD, occurring most often in eyes with pigment epithelial detachments (PEDs) and frequently temporally associated with initiation of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy. Improved recognition of high-risk PED features may enhance counseling and risk stratification.
This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines and was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251075278). MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL were searched from inception to December 15, 2025. Randomized and non-randomized studies of adults (≥50 years) with nAMD evaluating candidate predictors of RPE tear were included. Two reviewers independently screened, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias (RoB 2, ROBINS-I). Random-effects meta-analyses were performed for baseline PED height and area, with per-study weighting, standardized mean difference (SMD), and leave-one-out sensitivity analyses. Certainty was assessed using GRADE.
Sixteen studies comprising 4,043 eyes were included. Five studies (55 tear eyes; 370 controls; 425 eyes) showed greater baseline PED height in eyes developing tears (mean difference [MD], 212.39 µm; 95% confidence interval [CI], 133.87-290.91; I² = 24.6%; SMD, 1.09). Four studies (48 tear eyes; 1,312 controls; 1,360 eyes) showed larger baseline PED area (MD, 5.80 mm²; 95% CI, 3.48-8.12; I² = 2.7%; SMD, 0.88). Both persisted across all leave-one-out iterations; omitting the study contributing 43.5% of the area weight increased the effect (MD, 7.11 mm²). Fibrovascular PED, greater basal diameter and volume, subretinal fluid, sub-RPE clefts, radial hyperreflective lines, and occult or hemorrhagic macular neovascularization were also associated with tears. Anti-VEGF agent selection was not consistently associated. Most studies were at moderate to serious risk of bias; certainty was very low.
Baseline PED morphology, particularly increased height, is the most consistently reported association with RPE tear in nAMD. Because the evidence is predominantly retrospective, largely unadjusted, and of very low certainty, these are associations rather than validated predictive markers. Prospective studies using standardized imaging protocols are needed.

### 2. Association Between Systemic TNF-α Inhibitor Use and Risk of Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma.
- Authors: Alexander T Hong et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-22 | PMID: 42913929 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42913929/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.10.009

**Abstract (EN)**

To evaluate the association between systemic tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) inhibitor use and the long-term risk of primary open angle glaucoma (POAG), and to compare glaucoma risk among users of different TNF-α inhibitors.
Retrospective, multicenter clinical cohort study.
This retrospective, multicenter cohort study compared patients ≥40 years with and without TNF-α inhibitor use (2006-2026) using data from aggregated, de-identified electronic health records across 70 U.S. health care organizations. Patients prescribed TNF-α inhibitors (adalimumab, infliximab, etanercept, certolizumab pegol, or golimumab) were propensity matched (1:1) to controls without TNF-α inhibitor exposure based on demographics and systemic indications for TNF-a. Patients with a history of uveitis and related sequelae were excluded. New POAG diagnosis, identified by ICD-10 codes within 10 years of follow-up, was assessed using Cox proportional hazards models.
Among 12,227 TNF-α inhibitor users and 1,354,651 controls, 11,915 matched pairs were analyzed (mean [SD] age 59 [10] years; 68% female). At 10 years, TNF-α inhibitor users were associated with a lower risk of POAG diagnosis compared to controls (2.0% vs. 3.3%; HR 0.70 [95% CI 0.59-0.82]; P<.001) In agent-specific analysis, adalimumab-users (n=4,962 per cohort) (HR 0.74 [0.57-0.96]) and infliximab users (n=2,134 per cohort; HR 0.68 [95% CI 0.47-0.97) were associated with a lower risk of POAG diagnosis compared to matched controls. No significant differences were observed in pairwise comparisons amongst TNF-α inhibitors.
Systemic TNF-α inhibitor use, particularly adalimumab and infliximab, was associated with a modest but significant reduction in long-term POAG risk. These findings support a potential role of TNF-α-mediated inflammation in glaucomatous neurodegeneration.

### 2. OCT to Assess Risk of Progression of Multiple Sclerosis: A Report by the American Academy of Ophthalmology.
- Authors: Heather E Moss et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-21 | PMID: 42905039 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42905039/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ophtha.2026.08.009

**Abstract (EN)**

To review the current published literature on the utility of OCT of the retina in peripapillary and macular regions to predict or assess risk of disease activity in adult patients with multiple sclerosis (MS) and MS precursors (radiologically isolated syndrome and clinically isolated syndrome).
A literature search of the PubMed database was last performed in September 2025. Articles were limited to original research in the English language that included adult patients. The search yielded 275 articles that were examined in abstract form to exclude reviews, and 71 were reviewed in full text. Twenty-one articles were selected for inclusion in this assessment and were assigned a level of evidence rating by the panel methodologist.
Two studies were rated level I, 8 studies were rated level II, and 11 studies were rated level III. Baseline measurements (lower thickness, faster thickness decline, and larger interocular thickness differences) of the peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) and macular ganglion cell-inner plexiform layer (GC-IPL) from OCT scans in patients with MS and precursors of MS were associated with future progression of MS as measured by disability, clinical relapses, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) changes. Multiple variable models support the independent association of OCT metrics and MS progression.
Evidence supports the development of OCT-derived pRNFL and macular GC-IPL thickness measurements as biomarkers for future progression of MS in patients with MS or MS precursor syndromes. Future work is needed to improve generalizability by studying demographically heterogeneous cohorts and should include determining the comparability of metrics obtained from different OCT manufacturers' hardware and software. Future research is also needed to guide interpretation of OCT in the context of other predictors of future disease activity such as MRI.

### 1. Comparative Analysis of Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment Repair with or without Associated Macular Hole.
- Authors: Sihame Doukkali et al. | Date: 2026-Oct-23 | PMID: 42925163 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925163/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005038

**Abstract (EN)**

To assess the anatomic and functional outcomes in primary uncomplicated rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD) compared to RRD associated with macular hole (MH).
This retrospective cohort study included 1303 eyes with RRD and 30 eyes with MH-RRD operated on between 2014 and 2018 at a single tertiary center. Main outcomes were single-surgery anatomic success (SSAS), MH closure and final pinhole visual acuity (PHVA).
Of the 1333 patients, 867 (65%) were male, and median [Q1, Q3] age was 63 [57, 70]. Most of these were macula-off detachments (n=764, 57%) with a higher proportion in MH-RRD (n=26, 87%) than primary RRD (n=738, 57%) (p=0.004). Baseline VA was lower in MH-RRD (p<0.001). Postoperative PHVA at three months was 0.30 [0.18, 0.54] in primary RRD vs. 0.85 [0.60, 1.30] in MH-RRD (p<0.001); final PHVA was 0.18 [0.10, 0.40] vs. 0.68 [0.40, 0.90] (p<0.001) respectively. Primary MH closure was achieved in 27 (90%) MH-RRD cases; at final follow-up, all 30 MH-RRDs had attached retinas with one persistent MH. MH presence was not significantly associated with SSAS (p=0.391), while increasing age was a negative predictor of SSAS (p=0.008). Male sex (p<0.001), SF6 tamponade (p<0.001), SSAS (p<0.001), and longer follow-up (p<0.001) were positive predictors of final PHVA.
SSAS did not significantly differ between the two groups though the small MH-RRD sample may have contributed to this. Functional outcomes were poorer in MH-RRD, likely due to more macula-off RRD, more retinal breaks, occasional MH closure failure and residual sequelae related to the MH.
