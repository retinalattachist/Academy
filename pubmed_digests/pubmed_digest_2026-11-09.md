### 1. Baseline Macular OCT Thickness of Ocular Hypertensive Patients Improves Prediction of Glaucoma.
- Authors: Mauro Gobira et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-05 | PMID: 42987995 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42987995/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.11.003

**Abstract (EN)**

To determine whether baseline macular ganglion cell-inner plexiform layer (GCIPL) and circumpapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (cpRNFL) thickness add prognostic value for glaucomatous visual field (VF) damage in patients with ocular hypertension.
Retrospective clinical cohort study.
330 eyes of 235 patients with ocular hypertension, defined by intraocular pressure of 22 mmHg or higher or an International Classification of Diseases coded diagnosis, without repeatable glaucomatous VF damage at baseline. Eyes came from both the African Descent and Glaucoma Evaluation Study and a clinical cohort at the Shiley Eye Institute.
Baseline GCIPL and cpRNFL thickness were measured from the first good-quality Cirrus optical coherence tomography (OCT) macular and optic-disc cubes. Clinical predictors were assessed with the Ocular Hypertension Treatment Study (OHTS)-European Glaucoma Prevention Study (EGPS) Risk Calculator. Repeatable glaucomatous VF damage required a Glaucoma Hemifield Test outside normal limits or a pattern standard deviation at the 5% level or worse on three consecutive reliable Humphrey 24-2 examinations.
Hazard ratios per 1 standard deviation (SD) of thinner global and sectoral GCIPL or cpRNFL, and the change in the Harrell concordance (C) index (ΔC) after adding each measure to the Risk Calculator.
Glaucomatous VF damage developed in 47 of 330 eyes (14%) over a median 4.4 years. Adjusted for the Risk Calculator, thinner macular GCIPL was associated with conversion (hazard ratio [HR] 1.89 per 1-SD thinner; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.50 to 2.37; P < .001), and every macular sector was significant (HR 1.71 to 2.02). The Risk Calculator alone showed modest discrimination (Harrell C-index, 0.57). Adding macular GCIPL improved discrimination (ΔC +0.101; 95% CI, +0.022 to +0.220). Global cpRNFL thickness did not improve discrimination (ΔC +0.004; 95% CI, -0.021 to +0.133); among cpRNFL quadrants, only the inferior quadrant was associated with conversion (HR 1.50; 95% CI, 1.02 to 2.22).
In patients with ocular hypertension, thinner baseline macular GCIPL was associated with glaucomatous VF damage and improved the discrimination of the OHTS-EGPS Risk Calculator, whereas global cpRNFL thickness did not.

### 2. Statin Use and Age-related Macular Degeneration: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Disease Outcomes.
- Authors: Pedro Lucas Machado Magalhães et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-07 | PMID: 42998694 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42998694/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ajo.2026.11.005

**Abstract (EN)**

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the association between statin use and the prevalence, incidence, and progression of age-related macular degeneration (AMD).
AMD is a major cause of vision loss in older adults, many of whom receive statins for cardiovascular prevention.
We searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library through October 2025 for randomized trials and cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies with comparators. We assessed risk of bias with tools appropriate to each design and pooled effect measures separately using random-effects models. The protocol was registered in PROSPERO.
Twenty-eight studies included 1,918,120 participants. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) for the prevalence of any, early, and late AMD were 1.03 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.86-1.24), 1.10 (95% CI, 0.92-1.31), and 0.75 (95% CI, 0.46-1.25), respectively. For incidence of any AMD, the pooled hazard ratio (HR) was 1.04 (95% CI, 0.82-1.31) and the OR was 0.76 (95% CI, 0.43-1.34), with substantial heterogeneity. For neovascular AMD incidence, different study subsets suggested lower hazard (HR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.60-0.85) and higher risk (risk ratio, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.18-1.44); the OR was inconclusive (0.93; 95% CI, 0.82-1.05). Progression evidence was limited and heterogeneous. Ten of 12 longitudinal nonrandomized studies had serious overall risk of bias.
The evidence was of very low certainty and insufficient to establish a protective or harmful effect of statins on AMD. These findings do not support prescribing statins specifically for AMD or discontinuing otherwise indicated therapy.

### 1. Short-term Risk of Inflammatory Eye Reactions Following Bisphosphonate Use: A nationwide case-case-time-control study.
- Authors: Miyuki Hsing-Chun Hsieh et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-04 | PMID: 42982279 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982279/) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ophtha.2026.10.036

**Abstract (EN)**

To evaluate short-term associations between bisphosphonate exposure and uveitis, scleritis, and cystoid macular edema (CME).
Nationwide case-case-time-control (CCTC) study.
Patients with incident uveitis, scleritis, or CME identified from Taiwan's national health claims data (2011-2021), with matched future cases serving as controls to adjust for temporal exposure trends.
Bisphosphonate exposure during the 30-day hazard window immediately preceding each event was compared with exposure during an earlier 30-day reference window, separated by a 90-day washout period. Each case was matched to a future case to account for temporal trends in bisphosphonate use. Analyses were stratified by administration route and individual agent. Sensitivity analyses varied window lengths, washout periods, and lag structures. Denosumab and teriparatide served as negative-control exposures.
Incident uveitis, scleritis, and CME, with associations quantified using CCTC odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).
We identified 269,545 incident uveitis cases, 132,585 scleritis cases, and 115,914 CME cases; over 96% were matched to a future case and included in CCTC analyses. After accounting for exposure trends, any bisphosphonate use was associated with an increased short-term risk of uveitis (OR, 1.24; 95% CI, 1.00-1.56), driven primarily by anterior uveitis (OR 1.43; 95% CI, 1.10-1.86). In analyses stratified by administration route and individual agent, intravenous bisphosphonates (OR, 1.78; 95% CI, 1.22-2.61), particularly zoledronic acid (OR, 2.82; 95% CI, 1.70-4.67), were associated with increased uveitis risk. Oral bisphosphonates were not associated with uveitis (OR, 0.99; 95% CI, 0.74-1.33). No clear associations were observed for scleritis or CME. Findings were consistent across subgroup and sensitivity analyses, and associations for negative-control exposures were null.
Short-term associations between bisphosphonate use and ocular inflammatory events varied by administration route and outcome. Increased uveitis risk was concentrated among intravenous formulations, particularly zoledronic acid, whereas no clear associations were found for scleritis or CME. These findings may inform monitoring for ocular symptoms among patients receiving intravenous bisphosphonates.

### 1. Exploration of Potential Associations Between Sulfated Polysaccharide Exposure and Retinal Findings.
- Authors: Umut Akova et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-05 | PMID: 42987191 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42987191/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005047

**Abstract (EN)**

Pentosan polysulfate sodium (PPS), a widely used treatment for interstitial cystitis, has been linked to a distinctive macular toxicity. A recent case report described a similar phenotype with fondaparinux, a related sulfated polysaccharide, raising the possibility of a broader class effect. We explored potential associations between exposure to non-PPS sulfated polysaccharides and retinal imaging findings resembling PPS maculopathy.
We conducted a single center retrospective study from 2018-2024. Emory Healthcare electronic health records were queried for adults with exposure to fondaparinux, enoxaparin, chondroitin containing products, alginate containing products, and/or dalteparin. Macular imaging during the study period was required for inclusion. A retina specialist reviewed macular imaging for features resembling PPS maculopathy. Given the high number of enoxaparin users with short exposure durations, imaging review for this group was limited to >1 year of documented exposure.
A total of 218 patients were evaluated, including patients exposed to alginate (n = 12), fondaparinux (n = 25), chondroitin sulfate (n = 111), and enoxaparin (n = 70). Mean age at first qualifying macular imaging was 67.4 years and 59.5% were women. There were no cases resembling classic PPS maculopathy. Some patients did exhibit subtle macular pigment alterations without a clear diagnosis [fondaparinux 1 (4.0%); chondroitin sulfate 3 (2.7%); alginate 1 (8.3%)].
This study did not identify a relationship between non-PPS sulfated polysaccharide exposure and the development of macular disease resembling classic PPS maculopathy. Larger studies with increased power may be warranted to determine whether a rare association may be present.

### 2. COMPARISON OF TRACTION FORCES IN EX VIVO VITRECTOMY USING 25+®G 20K CPM DUAL-BLADE AND 25+®G 10K CPM SINGLE-BLADE VITRECTOMY PROBES.
- Authors: Steve Charles et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-06 | PMID: 42993209 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42993209/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005048

**Abstract (EN)**

To compare vitreous traction forces generated by a 25+® gauge (G) 20,000 cuts per minute (CPM) dual-blade vitrectomy probe (HYPERVIT®) and a 25+®G 10,000 CPM single-blade vitrectomy probe (Advanced ULTRAVIT®) under flow-matched conditions.
Bovine vitreous was used in a simulated ex vivo vitrectomy model. Each probe (n=10 per type) engaged vitreous within a balanced salt solution (BSS®) chamber, while a force sensor recorded traction forces. Vacuum levels were set at 260 mmHg (dual-blade) and 340 mmHg (single-blade) to achieve flow-matching, and traction forces were measured across percentiles.
Under flow-matched conditions, the dual-blade probe exhibited lower traction forces. Excluding forces <0.5 millinewton (mN), the 90th, 95th, and 99th percentile forces were 12.8%, 14.4%, and 14.4% lower, respectively (p<0.05). Overall mean, mean peak, and mean maximum forces were lowered by 8.01%, 15.78%, and 12.22%, respectively (p<0.05). The single-blade probe recorded traction forces of 1.60 ± 0.07 mN, 2.93 ± 0.14 mN, and 6.97 ± 0.38 mN, respectively. Whereas the dual-blade probe showed 1.47 ± 0.04 mN, 2.46 ± 0.12 mN, and 6.12 ± 0.26 mN (p<0.05).
The 25+G 20K CPM dual-blade probe generated lower traction forces than the 25+G 10K CPM single-blade probe under flow-matched conditions.

### 3. Post-Pars Plana Vitrectomy Endophthalmitis in Patients with and without Systemic Immunosuppression.
- Authors: Tushar Talaparthy et al. | Date: 2026-Nov-06 | PMID: 42993189 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42993189/) | DOI: 10.1097/IAE.0000000000005052

**Abstract (EN)**

To determine whether systemic immunosuppression is associated with endophthalmitis after pars plana vitrectomy (PPV), and whether risk varies by drug class and time window.
Retrospective propensity score matched cohort study using TriNetX, a federated network of 72 US healthcare organizations. Exposed adults undergoing PPV used corticosteroids, classic nonbiologic immunosuppressants, targeted small molecule immunomodulators, or biologics for at least 3 months before surgery; controls had none in the prior year. Matching controlled for demographics, comorbidities, and surgical indication. The outcome was endophthalmitis within 365 days, assessed over early (1-42 days), 1-90 day, and later (43-365 day) windows. Cox modeling was secondary.
After matching, 25,997 patients per cohort were analyzed. No class was associated with endophthalmitis during the early window. During the later window, risk was higher for all immunosuppressants (RR 1.74; 95% CI 1.33 to 2.28; P < .0001), corticosteroids (RR 1.81; 95% CI 1.38 to 2.37; P < .0001), and biologics (RR 2.00; 95% CI 1.00 to 4.03; P = .045). In Cox regression, corticosteroids (HR 1.19; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.39; P = .037) and biologics (HR 1.51; 95% CI 1.08 to 2.11; P = .016) remained independent risk factors.
Systemic corticosteroid use prior to PPV was associated with late-onset endophthalmitis, with excess risk appearing between 43 and 365 days rather than in the early period. This timing is atypical for surgically acquired infection, and some late events may reflect endogenous or inflammatory disease. Immunosuppressed patients may warrant a longer window of suspicion.
